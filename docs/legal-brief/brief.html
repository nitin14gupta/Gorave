2. The product in one page

Gorave helps adults (18+) in Indian cities find and join small social events hosted by other users: house parties, rooftop evenings, game nights, dinners, music sessions. Anyone can browse; verified users can host.

WhoWhat they do
GuestsBrowse events by city on a map or list, book a free spot (often by request) or buy a paid ticket, chat in the event's group chat, check in with a QR code, review the night, share photos to an event album, and connect with people they met ("vibes", which open a 1:1 chat).
HostsVerify their identity (PAN), sign the Host Agreement, add a payout account, create events (price, capacity, age limit, optional women-only / men-only / set ratio), approve requests, manage a waitlist, offer deals (early-bird price, promo codes), check guests in, and get paid after the event.
Gorave (the company)Runs the platform: listings, booking, payments (through Razorpay), chat, safety tools, moderation. We do not organise, own or attend events. We earn a ₹50 platform fee per paid ticket (paid by the guest) and a 10% commission on the ticket price (deducted from the host's payout).

Scale today: pre-launch; launching city by city starting with Mumbai.

3. Accounts, sign-in and SMS

3.1 Sign-in

  • Phone number + one-time code (OTP) only. No passwords, no social login. The OTP is sent by SMS through Twilio. On Android the code fills itself in from the SMS (Google SMS Retriever, no SMS-read permission).
  • Sessions use signed access and refresh tokens; signing out revokes them.
  • The user accepts the Terms and Privacy Policy on the phone-number screen ("By continuing you agree to our Terms and Privacy Policy, and to get event texts from Gorave").

3.2 Profile and age

  • Onboarding asks for: confirmation the user is 18+, name, date of birth (users under 18 are refused), gender (set once), photos, an optional 30-second voice intro, interests and city.
  • Age is self-declared at sign-up. Hosts' age is checked against their PAN record (section 4).

3.3 SMS and DLT

We send no promotional SMS. Marketing-style messages (a weekly "this weekend" digest) go only as in-app push notifications the user can switch off. Before launch we will register the company, our sender header and each template on a TRAI DLT platform (TCCCPR 2018) through our SMS provider. QUESTION Confirm that SOS alerts sent to a third party (the user's emergency contact, who has not signed up) are permissible as a service message.

3.4 Deleting an account

  • In the app: Settings → Account → Delete account, confirmed with an OTP. Also requestable by email.
  • A 30-day window to restore, then permanent deletion. Records the law requires (payments, tax, payouts) are kept.
  • A user cannot delete while hosting an upcoming event with guests (they must cancel first). Paid tickets for upcoming events are released without refund on deletion (warned in the flow). QUESTION Is releasing paid tickets without a refund on voluntary deletion acceptable, and what should happen to a remaining wallet balance (see 6.6)?

3.5 Locked accounts and appeals

  • Gorave staff can lock an account (for example after reports of harassment). A locked account is hidden from everyone else; the user sees the reason when they open the app.
  • One appeal per lock: a confirmation code, a short message, and a live selfie taken in the app, which a staff member compares with the profile photos. If the appeal is refused, the decision is final (they can still contact the Grievance Officer).
  • QUESTION The selfie is a face image used only for a human comparison (no automated face matching or template). Is any special consent or retention rule needed? We plan to delete appeal selfies 90 days after the decision.

4. Hosting

4.1 Who can host

  1. ID check (PAN). The user enters their PAN and agrees to the check. We verify it through Surepass (an authorised PAN verification API). The name and date of birth on the PAN record must match the profile; middle names are tolerated. A Gorave admin then approves. Two failed tries, after which they contact support. One PAN can be used on only one account.
  2. What we store: the result, the name and date of birth returned for the PAN, a masked PAN (last digits), and a one-way salted hash of the PAN (to block reuse). We do not store the full PAN. QUESTION Confirm this is a proportionate KYC record and what retention applies.
  3. Host Agreement (Appendix E): five short clauses shown as cards (keep guests safe, zero tolerance for harassment, reports are for real problems, follow local laws, the host is responsible), the full text one tap away, then the host draws a signature with their finger. We store the signature image and the acceptance time. QUESTION Is click-to-accept plus a drawn signature image sufficient evidence of acceptance (IT Act s.10A), and should we also store a hash of the agreement version accepted?
  4. Payout details: UPI ID or bank account in the host's name, stored encrypted.

5. Attending

  • Booking: instant join (paid events) or request-to-join (host approves; up to 2 requests per person per event). Full events have a waitlist; when a seat opens the next person gets 1 hour to take it.
  • Ticket: a QR code in the app. It can be passed to another Gorave user once (a "transfer"); it cannot be resold, and the receiver cannot cancel it for money. If the event is later cancelled, the refund goes to the person who paid.
  • Group chat: confirmed guests join the event's group chat; the host can pin messages, mute the chat or remove people.
  • After the event: reviews (stars and text), a shared photo album, and "People from last night" (the morning after, a list of who was there so you can send a vibe).
  • Guest Agreement (Appendix F) sets out conduct, ID at the door, photos, the host's role and liability.

6. Money

6.1 What a ticket costs and who gets what

Example: ₹599 ticketAmount
Guest pays: ticket + platform fee₹599 + ₹50 = ₹649
Gorave keeps: platform fee + 10% commission on the ticket₹50 + ₹60 = ₹110
Host receives₹599 − ₹60 = ₹539

The fee and commission rates are fixed per event at creation, so later changes never alter an existing event's maths.

6.2 Payments

  • Through Razorpay (UPI apps, UPI ID, UPI QR, cards), or the Gorave Wallet, or both. We never see or store card or UPI credentials.
  • Tickets are for real-world events, so on iOS/Android we use Razorpay, not Apple/Google in-app purchase (see section 10).
  • Money is collected into the company's Razorpay account. Host payouts are made manually today; we plan to automate with Razorpay Route/RazorpayX. QUESTION Under RBI's Payment Aggregator rules, does collecting on behalf of hosts and paying them out later require an escrow/nodal arrangement on our side, or is that handled by Razorpay as the PA? Any limit on how long we may hold host money?

6.3 Deals (early bird and promo codes)

  • Early bird: the first N tickets at a lower price. Limits: at least half the full price, for at most half the capacity.
  • Promo codes (e.g. RIYA20): % or ₹ off the ticket price, optional use limit. Limit: up to 50% off; deeper discounts (up to 100%) only for codes limited to 10 uses (guest lists).
  • Deals don't stack; a guest gets the cheaper one. The ₹50 fee is never discounted. The host funds the discount: payouts are always based on the price actually paid.
  • Counters shown to guests ("Early bird · 3 left", "5 spots left") are live, true numbers. QUESTION Please confirm our "was ₹1,299 / now ₹908" display and urgency messages are compliant with the CCPA Guidelines on Dark Patterns 2023 (false urgency, drip pricing): the fee is shown before payment.

6.5 How refunds reach the guest

  • A refund lands in the guest's Gorave Wallet instantly and never expires. The guest then chooses: keep it in the wallet, or send the card/UPI part back to the original payment method (5 to 7 business days; Razorpay refund). This is possible for about 180 days after the payment (the gateway's limit); after that, through support by bank transfer.

6.6 The Gorave Wallet

  • Holds only refunds. No top-ups, no transfers between users, no interest, no cash withdrawal except sending a refund back to the payment it came from.
  • Spendable only on Gorave tickets. We believe this is a closed-system PPI outside RBI licensing.
  • QUESTION Please confirm the closed-system characterisation, including the "send back to source" feature. QUESTION On account deletion our draft says any remaining balance is lost after 30 days. We would prefer to require the user to withdraw it to source first, or refund it automatically. What is the correct position under consumer law?

6.7 Host payouts

  • After the event, if there are no open reports, the host is paid the ticket prices actually paid minus 10%, for tickets not refunded. Target timing: within [7] business days DECIDE.
  • Payouts can be held while reports are investigated. If an event is found to be fake or misleading, guests are refunded in full and the host is not paid; past payouts may be recovered from future ones (Host Agreement).

8. Messaging, content and notifications

  • 1:1 chat opens when a "vibe" (a short intro message) is accepted. Text, photos, videos, GIFs, voice notes, shared events and profiles; edit and unsend; reactions; view-once/twice media.
  • Media storage: chat media sits in a private storage bucket (Cloudflare R2) reachable only by short-lived signed links. View-once media is deleted 10 minutes after the last allowed view; unopened ones after 14 days.
  • Event group chats, event albums (photos after the event, removable and reportable) and reviews are other user content.
  • Push notifications (Expo → Apple/Google push) in five categories the user can switch off: Social, Hosting, Events you're going to, Payments, and Picks for you (weekend digest, saved event filling up). No marketing email or SMS.
  • We do not run third-party advertising or sell data.

12. Questions for counsel

  1. Status. Are we an intermediary under the IT Act, a marketplace e-commerce entity under the E-Commerce Rules, or both, and what follows (seller information, grievance timelines, safe harbour conditions)?
  2. Event liability. Hosts are independent organisers. Are our limitation of liability and host indemnity (Appendices A, E, F) enforceable and sufficient? Should hosts carry event insurance?
  3. Wallet. Is the refund-only, closed, send-back-to-source wallet exempt from RBI PPI licensing (6.6)?
  4. Wallet on deletion. What must happen to a balance when a user deletes their account?
  5. Refund terms. Is keeping the ₹50 fee on a guest's own cancellation, and no refund inside 48 hours, enforceable and fair under the Consumer Protection Act?
  6. Payment flow. RBI Payment Aggregator rules for collecting on behalf of hosts and paying out later (6.2).
  7. Tax. GST on fee/commission; TCS s.52; TDS s.194-O; invoices (6.8).
  8. KYC. PAN verification through Surepass: consent wording, what we may store, retention (4.1).
  9. Gender-restricted events. Women-only and men-only events, and seats offered by gender, are core to safety for many users. Any discrimination or other risk, and how should the Terms frame it?
  10. Appeal selfies. Consent and retention for a face image used for a manual identity check (3.5).
  11. SOS. Sending a user's location to a third party at their request (3.3).
  12. Deals and urgency. Dark-patterns compliance of our deal displays and scarcity counters (6.3).
  13. Alcohol and minors. Events may serve alcohol; drinking ages differ by state. Is the host allocation plus our 18+ rule and host age limits enough?
  14. Music. Is placing PPL/IPRS licensing on hosts adequate?
  15. DPDP consent. The exact notice and consents we need at sign-up and for optional permissions (9.4).
  16. Contract formation. Validity of click-wrap acceptance and drawn signatures; should we store accepted document versions (4.1)?
  17. Grievance Officer. Must it be a founder/employee resident in India? Can one person also be the DPDP contact?
  18. Website. Public event pages and public member pages (gorave.in/u/username) show a safe subset of profile data. Is that consistent with our Privacy Policy and DPDP?
  19. Anything else you would change in the product before launch.
